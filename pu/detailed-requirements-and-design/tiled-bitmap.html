Tiled bitmap¶

Scroom visualizes bitmaps that are so large they do not (or at least they used to) fit comfortably in memory. And even if it did fit, scaling down a multi-gigabyte bitmap to a size that fits on your screen would take significant amounts of cpu time. To work around this, Scroom stores your image in pre- scaled layers, each layer a factor 8 smaller than the one below it. Layers are then further subdivided into tiles, such that only the area of the image you are currently looking at needs to be in memory.

When you first open a bitmap, there’s a preprocessing phase, in which all tiles in all layers are initialized, scaling down the original bitmap as needed.

Requirements¶

There’s really only one: Render large bitmaps as faithfully as possible, as fast as possible. See be fast. The pre-scaling we do into different layers gives rise to a ridiculous amount of coordinate systems. To make things somewhat manageable, all coordinate systems share the same origin (with a few exceptions, unfortunately). Let’s go over all coordinate systems:

Presentation coordinates

These are inherited from Scroom. They are the true-size coordinates, without any scaling. The bitmap might be located at an offset, though. Presentation coordinates are identical to the coordinates of the bottom most layer.

Source image coordinates

These are coordinates within the bitmap being loaded, at true-size. They are Presentation coordinates, minus the offset (if any).

View coordinates

These are coordinates of the destination window. It has, by necessity, a different origin, and it is scaled. When scroom calls our redraw method, it wants us to draw the given presentationArea at the requested zoomfactor, such that the top left of the presentationArea lands at (0, 0). The size of the viewArea follows from scaling presentationArea.size() up or down as requested.

Layer coordinates

These are coordinates inside one of the pre-scaled layers. They are Presentation coordinates, but with x and y scaled with the zoomfactor belonging to the layer

Grid coordinates or tile indices

These coordinates are are for indexing tiles within a layer. For positive x and y, they’d be Layer coordinates div the tile size. Because div rounds towards zero, this breaks for negative coordinates, but you get the picture

Tile coordinates

These are coordinates within a given tile. The origin is the top left corner of the tile. So for positive x and y, they’d be Layer coordinates mod the tile size. Again, this unfortunately breaks down for negative coordinates

Naming conventions¶

In many cases, the coordinate system will be obvious. presentationArea is an area in presentation coordinates. viewArea is in view coordinates. In case of ambiguity, for example after coordinate conversions, we suffix variable names with an InXXXCoordinates. For example, presentationAreaInLayerCoordinates might be a useful thing to have. Though here too, there might be sane defaults. Like a layer might take layer coordinates by default, and a tile might use tile coordinates. No need for suffixes there.

Conversion functions are typically called xxxCoordinatesFromYyyCoordinates(). This matches well with the naming conventions for variables. A line might read:

auto tileIndex = tileIndexFromLayerCoordinates(areaInLayerCoordinates);
auto areaInTileCoordinates = tileCoordinatesFromLayerCoordinates(tileIndex, areaInLayerCoordinates);

Note how, lexically, the two tileCoordinates are close together, just as the two layerCoordinates. That makes for easy reviewing
